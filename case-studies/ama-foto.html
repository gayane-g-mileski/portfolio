01 — Context

EU rules say Austria's farmers must document their fields every year. In 2021 there was still no way to do that out in the field.

To claim their subsidies, farmers have to send in photos of each parcel once a year. It is not optional — get it wrong and a farm can lose thousands of euros.

Until 2021 all of it went through the AMA web portal, built for desktop and for filling in applications. So farmers drove out, took photos on their phones, then uploaded them later at home. Two separate jobs, easy to get wrong, and no help at all while standing in the field.

AMA Foto was the answer to that: the first tool built for the job itself. There was nothing to iterate on — this workflow had never had proper software. Nearly a quarter of photo packages came in with mistakes that AMA staff had to sort out by hand, costing the authority around €800,000 a year and holding up payments to farmers.

What we were up against: users from 25 to 75, standing in a field with little or no signal, often in work gloves, in a hurry because the season waits for nobody, in whatever weather. It had to work as reliably as a paper form and be far quicker to get right.

02 — Research

So we went out to the fields. Literally.

Our researchers spent three weeks visiting farms in Lower Austria, Burgenland and Tyrol. Not usability testing in a room — watching farmers use the app while standing in the field, sun in their eyes, trying to photograph forty hectares.

User Research — Affinity Mapping

Five farmers took us through their year — Hans (54, dairy), Maria (42, crops), Johann (38, organic), Elisabeth (61, livestock) and Thomas (29, livestock). Sixty separate frustrations came out of it.

Synthesis — Three Root Problems

Sorted, they were not sixty complaints but three recurring problems. Farmers were forced back and forth between the field and the computer at home. Workarounds patched the symptoms and left the cause. And half-finished applications sent people to the AMA phone line, which made trouble for everyone downstream.

Affinity Clustering — Two Iteration Passes

Two rounds of sorting turned that into ten things we could act on — first the access problems, like desktop-only screens, fiddly logins, no signal and gloves; then the field problems, like photo quality, knowing which parcel you are on, GPS accuracy, working offline and checking a submission on the spot.

Decision 01

Contextual Field Detail — map, obligations, and data in one view

Open a parcel and you see its boundary on the satellite map, the crop registered against it, and anything still outstanding as a card you can act on — a missing flowering date, say, sitting right at the bottom of the screen. Everything about this field, in one place.

Satellite map with exact parcel polygon overlay
Pending obligations surfaced as contextual action cards
Four focused tabs: Auftrag, Details, Unterlagen, Korrektur
Decision 02

Structured Correction Flow — review changes before they're submitted

When something needs changing — crop type, parcel code, greening variant — the correction tab shows plainly what it was and what it will become. Confirm or discard, and nothing goes to AMA until you say so. A comment can be added, so there is always a record of why.

Impact
The from/to structure replaced ambiguous free-form edits. Documentation errors dropped from 23% → 2.8%, reducing manual AMA review workload by 78% and saving €620,000 annually.
Decision 03

Priority-Driven Task Queue — deadlines visible before you set off

In peak season an inspector can be carrying hundreds of open jobs. The task list puts the urgent ones on their own, counts down the deadlines next to the distance, and labels what each one is for. Sort by date, distance or type and the day's route plans itself.
