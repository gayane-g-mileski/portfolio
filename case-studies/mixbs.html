01 — Brief

MiXBS runs outsourced contact centres out of the US, and has done for fifteen years — DesignRush puts them in Florida's top ten. They build remote teams for customer service, marketing support and back-office work, across logistics, healthcare, retail and events.

BayCX is their own software, and everything runs on it. Agents and team leads use it to handle calls, chats and workflows in one place — up to 16,000 conversations a day for clients like CDEK, in several languages.

I came in as a consultant from 2022 to 2025. My job was to work out what the platform needed and in what order, then see it designed: the module map, the visual direction, the design system, and signing in on mobile.

02 — System Architecture

Nothing could be designed before we understood how the platform actually fits together. BayCX runs everything through one coordinating layer — scheduling, call handling, tickets, contacts and chat, plus the plumbing underneath for messages, sign-in and reporting.

That map settled a lot of arguments. It showed which parts drive signing in, how a session travels through the system, and where a conversation is handed from one channel to the next. Out of it came the sign-in flow: log in, forgot my password, one-time code, new password.

03 — Visual Exploration

Before committing to a look, we put five colour schemes on exactly the same screens — the teal we already favoured, plus purple, navy, maroon and green.

Same layout every time, so the only thing anyone was judging was the colour. Everyone could see the options side by side and choose; the teal won, and became the platform's.

04 — Design System

The design system settles the colours once and for all: a teal family and an orange family, five steps each, a nine-step grey for surfaces and text, and plain black and white. After that nobody has to invent a shade.

The button covers everything anyone might need — five sizes, six variants, both colour families, light and dark backgrounds. Developers never have to guess, and the screens come out consistent.
