Wien Miete — What May This Flat Rent For?

A fast first assessment of what a Vienna flat may lawfully be let for — the rent type, how far the MRG reaches, and the customary market band. Built for landlords and investors, and readable enough that a tenant can check the same figure from the other side.

01 — Brief

Before a Vienna flat is listed, refurbished or bought, one number decides the case: what it may lawfully be let for. Getting it wrong is expensive in both directions — set it too high and the rent is challengeable years later; set it too low and the yield is gone.

Wien Miete returns a fast first assessment of three things at once: which rent type applies, how far the Mietrechtsgesetz reaches, and what the customary market band is.

Landlords & investors

The primary audience. They need a defensible ceiling before they list or buy, and a paper trail if it is ever questioned.

Tenants

The same calculation read from the other side: not "what may I charge" but "what may I be charged". Fewer of them, so they follow the landlord flow rather than getting their own.

02 — Which law applies

Everything downstream hangs on one branch: how far the Mietrechtsgesetz applies. That is fixed by the building permit date and the contract date — facts neither side has to hand. The app infers the regime from ordinary questions, then names it.

03 — The rent-check workflow

The flow is ordered by what the calculation needs next, not by what is easy to ask. Each stage shows its effect immediately, so the verdict never arrives as a surprise — and where an answer is missing, it loops back and asks for exactly that gap.

Both audiences walk the same path. Only the framing of the result changes: a ceiling to set, or a figure to check.

04 — Before and after

The comparison that mattered was against what people do today — piecing a figure together from the contract, comparable listings and an advice page, and ending with an estimate nobody trusts enough to act on.

05 — Information architecture

The check is one journey, but it has to be re-enterable — people stop when they cannot find a document and return later. Everything else exists to support that: saved objects, plain-language explanations, and the documents produced at the end.
