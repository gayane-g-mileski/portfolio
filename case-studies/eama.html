01 — Challenge

The portal that hands out billions in EU farm payments had never really been designed. It had accumulated.

eAMA.at is where 165,000 farms meet the state: the big annual application, the parcel and crop declarations, the environmental programmes, the livestock records. Fifteen years of adding things one at a time had left four parts that looked and worked nothing like each other.

You could see the cost every spring, in the six weeks when all 165,000 farms are up against the same deadline. The helpline filled up with people asking where to click, not whether they qualified — and consultants were filling in something like a third of all applications for farmers who could not get through it alone.

On a phone it was worse. The map where you draw and confirm your field boundaries only worked on a desktop computer, which locked farmers out of the one step they would naturally do standing in the field.

Before & After

From modules without a system

Fifteen years of adding pieces had left eAMA as four loosely joined parts, each with its own menu, its own typefaces, its own look. They are now one portal with one design behind it: a single tabbed area, a login you cannot miss, and market prices down the side.

02 — Research

Ten weeks out on farms, in AMA offices and at agricultural advisers — three regions, two growing seasons.

This user base makes ordinary research methods useless. A wide age range with plenty of farmers over 55, all the pressure crammed into a six-week window, very mixed confidence with technology, and the actual places the work happens: barns, fields, muddy hands. A lab booking was never going to tell us anything.

Farm-Site Contextual Inquiry
32 on-farm observation sessions across Niederösterreich, Steiermark, and Burgenland. Captured real interaction patterns including glove use, sunlight, and tablet positioning in the field.
Consultant Interviews
18 interviews with agricultural consultants (Landwirtschaftskammer advisors) who regularly complete MFA applications on behalf of clients. Surface-level proxy for farmer intent data.
Helpdesk Call Analysis
Qualitative analysis of 800 transcribed helpdesk calls from MFA season 2020. Categorised by root cause — 73% were navigation or orientation failures, not process or eligibility questions.
Accessibility Audit
Full WCAG 2.1 review against AA criteria. Screen reader testing with NVDA and VoiceOver. Over 3,000 violations catalogued — priority-weighted against the MFA submission flow.
Analytics Review
Two-year session data review. Identified the exact steps in the MFA flow where drop-off clustered, peak-hour traffic patterns during deadline week, and the 14 most common error-state entries.

Key Research Findings

1
The MFA flow spanned 4 separate modules with no shared state
Farmers had to navigate between the parcel declaration module, the crop declaration module, the ÖPUL measure selector, and the final submission screen — each with its own URL, session, and navigation chrome. If they lost their place, there was no "where was I" mechanism. 44% of sessions during MFA season involved at least one module re-entry after abandonment.
2
Agricultural consultants had become a workaround for the portal's usability debt
Interviews with Landwirtschaftskammer advisors revealed that completing eAMA applications on behalf of clients had become a routine service — not for complex edge cases, but for standard MFA submissions. One consultant described completing applications for 120 clients annually, spending an average of 35 minutes per farm. This represented a structural cost invisible to AMA's usability metrics.
3
The GIS parcel viewer — the most critical component — was desktop-only
54% of sessions were on mobile or tablet, and usage was growing year-on-year. The parcel boundary tool, which farmers use to confirm and adjust their field geometries, was built on a desktop mapping library with no touch event handling. On tablets in the field — the most natural context for parcel confirmation — the interaction was completely non-functional.
4
Over 3,000 WCAG violations — 12 critical, concentrated in the highest-traffic flows
The most critical: form labels missing on 14 MFA input fields, colour contrast failures across table cells (contrast ratio 2.8:1 against the green header), keyboard traps in the parcel selector, and a base font size of 12px throughout the data tables. Given the user base age profile — approximately 40% of active farms operated by those over 55 — these were not edge cases.
02b — IA Audit

Before redesigning anything we mapped every screen the old portal could put in front of you — the full size of what a farmer was expected to find their way through.

The audit put numbers on what we had only suspected: eight top-level modules, no shared way of getting around, and more than 140 separate screens between a farmer and a submitted application.

03 — Process Design

Four disconnected parts became one application you can stop and come back to — without touching the regulated data underneath.

The awkward part was that those four parts were not an accident. Parcels, crops, environmental measures and the final acknowledgement really are run by separate systems inside AMA. The front had to become one thing while every one of those boundaries stayed exactly where it was.

So I ran a three-day workshop with AMA's product, technical and legal people to map what actually depends on what, and to find where we could reveal things step by step without creating a compliance problem.

Redesigned MFA Submission Flow

Twenty-five steps, several of them printing a PDF and posting it to AMA and then waiting for a reply, became seven steps you can finish online in one sitting.

Before — paper-bound
25 steps · 5 PDF printouts · 5 postal returns · 4 blocking waits
online step print PDF post to AMA wait for AMA
  1. 01Log in to eAMA
  2. 02Open MFA module
  3. 03Select farm & season
  4. 04Enter parcel list
  5. 05Print parcel list (PDF)
  6. 06Sign & post to AMA
  7. 07Wait for parcel registration
  8. 08Log back in
  9. 09Open GIS module (desktop only)
  10. 10Draw field boundaries
  11. 11Print GIS sketch (PDF)
  12. 12Post sketch to AMA
  13. 13Wait for boundary confirmation
  14. 14Re-open MFA module
  15. 15Declare crops per parcel
  16. 16Print crop declaration (PDF)
  17. 17Post declaration + documents
  18. 18Open ÖPUL module separately
  19. 19Select agri-environment measures
  20. 20Print ÖPUL commitment (PDF)
  21. 21Sign & post ÖPUL form
  22. 22Wait for eligibility check
  23. 23Print final MFA summary (PDF)
  24. 24Post signed summary to AMA
  25. 25Wait for confirmation letter
After — fully online
7 steps · no printing · no postal returns · no waiting
  1. 01Farm login (single sign-on)
  2. 02Farm & season — pre-filled from last year
  3. 03Parcel map — touch-first GIS, draw on device
  4. 04Crop declaration — per parcel, validated live
  5. 05ÖPUL measures — optional, save & return
  6. 06Review & submit — qualified electronic signature
  7. 07Instant confirmation + AMA-signed PDF receipt
Result: five PDF printouts, five postal returns and four blocking waits — the weeks of dead time between them — eliminated entirely. Average active submission time for returning users fell from 14.8 to 6.3 minutes, now completed in a single session.
Design System

A government-grade component
library built for longevity

The design language writes down every component in every state it can be in — normal, hover, focus, pressed, disabled, error — with the contrast checked against the accessibility standard and German labels matching the live portal. The same library went on to carry the AMA Foto app and the Rindernet app.

"The redesign fundamentally changed the dynamic between our farmers and the portal. For the first time, we saw farmers completing the MFA independently who had relied on advisors for years. That's the measure we care about most."
