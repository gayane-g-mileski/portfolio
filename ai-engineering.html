Product Management

Running a product

The parts of the job I own on every engagement — deciding what gets built and why, keeping a team aligned around it, and holding the release bar.

Requirements into Backlog

Read the specifications, the legal texts and the stakeholder input, and turn them into a backlog the team can size and commit to. AI takes the first pass through the long documents; the call on what matters stays mine.

RequirementsBacklogAI-assisted

Deciding Before Building

Get the decision while it is still cheap to change. A clickable concept in days rather than a slide deck, so a steering group approves or rejects something real before the budget is committed.

ConceptStakeholdersGo / No-Go

Evidence for Prioritisation

What goes to the top of the backlog rests on evidence — survey data, usability testing, support volumes and drop-off rates — rather than on who argued hardest in the room.

EvidencePrioritisationMetrics

Scale Assumptions

Specify for the range the product will actually meet — a single farmer against a large retailer. Volumes and peak loads settled while they are still cheap, not discovered at go-live.

VolumesSpecification

Scope and Dependencies

Map the whole product as a hierarchy of use cases, so scope, dependencies and the gaps between teams are visible and arguable before anything is committed to a release date.

ScopeDependenciesPlanning

Built Against Agreed

Check what was built against what was agreed while the sprint is still open — the UI against the spec, the behaviour against the acceptance criteria — so gaps are a conversation with the team, not a finding at acceptance.

AcceptanceDeliveryTeam

Edge Cases and Risk

Enumerate every state, edge case and failure a workflow can reach — AI is good at the exhaustive list — so each one is costed and decided on deliberately, instead of found by a user in production.

Edge CasesRiskAI-assisted

One Standard Across the Portfolio

Hold several products in one family to a single standard: shared components, the same accessibility bar, one definition of done — and push a change through all of them at once.

PortfolioStandardsAccessibility

The Decision Record

Keep the decisions, their rationale and the open questions written down and current, so a new team member — or an auditor — can trace why the product is the way it is.
