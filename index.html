Taste

Taste is doing and trying. Making something that does not work, trying the next thing, failing, and doing it better on the project after that. Not consuming vast amounts of good design and best practice.

It causes friction — and then the friction becomes the curriculum. It is slow, mostly invisible, and most of the time nobody asks for it: people will consume anything, and at first glance the output often looks no different.

That is frustrating. But once every other skill has been outsourced, taste is the one thing left. Everybody can produce anything now. Nobody can tell me what is worth showing.

How I Work

What I take responsibility for

01

Strategy and roadmap

Deciding what gets built and why. Framing the problem with the business, sequencing releases against legal deadlines and budget cycles, and being clear about what is not being built.

02

Delivery ownership

Running a product from discovery through release and onwards — embedded with the team, close enough to engineering that the intent survives implementation.

03

Stakeholder alignment

Building agreement across policy, legal, technical and communications teams — and carrying business departments through changes they did not ask for (this is the hardest part, of course).

04

The quality bar

Research and usability testing to settle the arguments, and WCAG 2.1 AA as a release condition rather than an afterthought. The UX groundwork under the product decisions.
